# Cyc

Cyc is a large hand-built knowledge base of common-sense facts and rules, begun in the 1980s and developed by Cycorp. This entry gives its definition and dated history, and records the two start years found in the sources.

## Definition

Lenat describes Cyc as "an expert system with a domain that spans all everyday objects and actions". In 1995 he reported a universal schema of roughly 10^5 general concepts and approximately 10^6 hand-entered common-sense axioms, built by "a person-century of effort" [1]. The goal was to supply the background knowledge that language understanding and learning programs lacked, so that [knowledge acquisition](./knowledge-acquisition-bottleneck.md) could later proceed from text [1]. Cycorp's site now describes Cyc as "Machine Reasoning AI that uses codified human common sense and knowledge" (first-party) [2].

## How it changed

- **1984.** Lenat and Mary Shepherd begin the work, per Lenat [1]. Cycorp's history page says Lenat founded the project at MCC in 1984 (first-party) [3].
- **1985.** Lenat, Prakash and Shepherd publish the Cyc paper in *AI Magazine* 6(4), the Winter 1985 issue [4][7]; the day is disputed (see below). Era: [Knowledge systems](../history/knowledge-systems.md).
- **End of 1994.** The project leaves MCC and becomes Cycorp, Inc. (first-party) [3].
- **1995.** Lenat's CACM 38(11) article gives the size above [1]. Era: [The Web changes data and search](../history/the-web.md).
- **July 2001 and 2002.** OpenCyc is announced (July 2001) and first released (2002); the last version, 4.0 in mid-2012, has about 239,000 concepts (a blogger's account, not Cycorp's) [5].
- **About March 2017.** Cycorp withdraws OpenCyc; its site is quoted as calling the release "fragmented" [5]. Era: [Knowledge graphs at scale](../history/knowledge-graphs-at-scale.md).
- **2021.** Hogan et al. call Cyc proprietary, with over 900 person-years of effort and 2.2 million facts and rules [6].
- **2023.** Cycorp's page carries an In Memoriam notice for Lenat (1950-2023) (first-party) [3].
- **2025.** Cycorp's home page lists hospital-operations software and says "200+ Hospitals trust our software products" (first-party) [2].

## Where sources disagree

**The start year.** Lenat [1] and Cycorp [3] give 1984. Hogan et al. write that effort has gone into Cyc "since 1986" [6], and the passage gives no reason: **unstated**. The paper of 1985 [4] predates 1986, which fits 1984. The [era page](../history/knowledge-systems.md) shows both.

**The day of the paper.** The publisher dates the issue "Vol. 6 No. 4: Winter 1985" to 1985-12-15 [7]. Its record of the Cyc paper says 1985-03-15 [4], but four other articles of the issue say 1985-12-15 [8]. The Interlisp bibliography copies 1985-03-15 [9]; OpenAlex gives 1986-01-01 [10]. *Reading:* a March date does not fit a winter issue, so this entry gives the year, 1985; the day is **unstated**.
