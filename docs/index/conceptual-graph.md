# Conceptual graph

## Definition

A conceptual graph is a graph notation for logic proposed by John Sowa. His own page defines conceptual graphs as "a system of logic based on the existential graphs of Charles Sanders Peirce" [1]. In his survey of [semantic networks](./semantic-network.md) he calls them "a variety of propositional semantic networks" and says they combine features of Tesnière's dependency graphs with Peirce's existential graphs [2]. A concept box is a quantifier restricted to a type, such as Farmer or Donkey [2].

## How it changed

- 197607: Sowa, "Conceptual Graphs for a Data Base Interface", IBM Journal of Research and Development 20(4), pages 336-357 [3].
- 197906: Sowa, "Semantics of Conceptual Graphs", 17th Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics, pages 39-44 [4].
- 2007: Common Logic, ISO/IEC 24707, is dated 2007 in Sowa's reference list; per Sowa, the Conceptual Graph Interchange Format (CGIF) is specified by a translation to Common Logic [2]. The date of the CGIF specification is **unstated** in the sources read. Sowa names ISO/IEC 24707 as the official standard for conceptual graph syntax and semantics and calls the earlier ANSI draft obsolete [5]. The ISO page itself could not be read (HTTP 403), so the standard's dates and status are **unstated** here.

Hogan and colleagues count conceptual graphs, with [semantic networks](./semantic-network.md) and [frames](./frame.md), among the 1970s attempts to give diagrams a formal semantics, and "direct predecessors of Description Logics" [6]. The period is told in the era [Formal foundations](../history/formal-foundations.md).

## Where sources disagree

The year. Hogan and colleagues write "the proposal of conceptual graphs by Sowa (1979)" and cite the ACL paper [6]. Gutierrez and Sequeda and Sowa date them 1976 [2][7]; Sowa's database-interface paper is indeed from July 1976 [3]. *Reading:* each date is right for the paper it cites; no source read says which paper first proposed the notation.
