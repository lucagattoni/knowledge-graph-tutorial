# schema.org

This entry answers what schema.org is and how it grew from a 2011 search-engine initiative. It lists the dated steps from the announcement to the current release. It also records where the sources name the founders and the kind of thing it is differently.

## Definition

Schema.org describes itself as "a collaborative, community activity" whose mission is to create, maintain and promote schemas for structured data on the Internet [1][2]. Its vocabulary covers entities, relationships between entities and actions, and can be written in RDFa, Microdata or [JSON-LD](./json-ld.md) [2]. Its FAQ states that it is a collaboration between search engines and "not a formal standards body" [3]. "Standard" here means a shared, published vocabulary.

## How it changed

- **20110602.** Google, Bing and Yahoo! announce schema.org as a common vocabulary for structured data markup on web pages, with schemas for more than a hundred new categories. The announcement says the work draws on [Cyc](./cyc.md), [DBpedia](./dbpedia.md) and [linked data](./linked-data.md) (first-party) [4]. See [The semantic web and linked data](../history/semantic-web.md).
- **20121108 (release 0.99).** The GoodRelations e-commerce vocabulary is added [5].
- **20140818 (release 1.9).** The terms of service note that Yandex is now a Sponsor [5].
- **201504.** The W3C Schema.org Community Group becomes the main forum for schema collaboration [1].
- **2015.** A report cited by Hitzler finds schema.org annotations on over 30% of pages [6]. See [Knowledge graphs at scale](../history/knowledge-graphs-at-scale.md).
- **2024 and 2026.** The site states that over 45 million web domains use it, with over 450 billion objects, as of 2024 (first-party) [2]. Release 30.1 of 20260916 adds vocabulary for EU Digital Product Passports and for common retail product data [5].

## Where sources disagree

**Who founded it.** The 2011 announcement names Google, Bing and Yahoo! [4]. Hitzler writes that it was driven by Bing, Google and Yahoo! and "slightly later joined by Yandex" [6]. The About page lists the "founding companies" as Google, Microsoft, Yahoo and Yandex [1]. The FAQ names the same four, but writes Bing in one answer and Microsoft in another [3]. Release 1.9 of 20140818 calls Yandex a Sponsor [5].

**What kind of thing it is.** Hitzler calls it "a relatively simple ontology" [6]. The FAQ calls it a markup schema and notes that Microdata was a "pragmatic decision at the time", with RDFa and JSON-LD supported since [3].
