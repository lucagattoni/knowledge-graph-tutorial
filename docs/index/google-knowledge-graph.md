# Google Knowledge Graph

The Google Knowledge Graph is the entity graph behind Google Search, announced on 16 May 2012. The announcement gave the phrase "knowledge graph" its current use. This entry gives the definition and the dated changes.

## Definition

Google's announcement describes it as a model of real-world things and how they connect, which "understands real-world entities and their relationships to one another". The slogan was "things, not strings". At launch it held "more than 500 million objects" and "more than 3.5 billion facts about and relationships between" them, and it was "rooted in public sources such as Freebase, Wikipedia and the CIA World Factbook" (first-party) [1].

## How it changed

- **20120516.** Amit Singhal announces the Knowledge Graph [1]. Hogan et al. write that the modern use of the phrase "stems from" this announcement, although it appeared in the literature as early as 1972 [2]. Era: [Knowledge graphs at scale](../history/knowledge-graphs-at-scale.md).
- **20200520.** Danny Sullivan writes that it holds "over 500 billion facts about five billion entities", and that Google builds it from the web, open-source databases and licensed data (first-party) [3].
- **Until 2015.** [Freebase](./freebase.md), a main source, ran until 2015 and its API was shut down [4]. Its content partly moved to [Wikidata](./wikidata.md) [2].
- **Today.** The Knowledge Graph Search API returns single matching entities in schema.org types and JSON-LD, not linked subgraphs. Its page says Google is migrating it to Cloud Enterprise Knowledge Graph, gives no end date, and calls it unsuitable as a production-critical service (page updated 20240426; first-party) [5].

## Where sources disagree

No disagreement found in the sources read. The figures of 2012 and 2020 are Google's own, worded differently (objects against entities), and they are not a measured growth rate [1][3]. A current size is **unstated**.
