# Semantic network

This entry answers what a semantic network is, who started the idea, and what came of it. It follows the idea from machine-translation work in the 1950s to the formal systems of the 1970s and 1980s. It also records where the sources give different starting points.

## Definition

Sowa defines a semantic network as "a graph structure for representing knowledge in patterns of interconnected nodes and arcs". Computer versions were first built for artificial intelligence and machine translation; earlier versions come from philosophy, psychology and linguistics [1]. Sowa says no necessary and sufficient conditions separate semantic networks from other systems [1].

## How it changed

- **3rd century AD.** Sowa reports the oldest known semantic network, drawn by Porphyry [1].
- **1956.** Richens's proposal for "preprogramming" a machine-translation system is, per Sowa, the first published use of the term ("semantic net") [1]. Gutierrez and Sequeda also date semantic networks to Richens, 1956 [2]. See [The advent](../history/advent.md).
- **1961 to 1966.** Masterman's Cambridge group organised 100 primitive concept types in a lattice [1]. Quillian's 1963 paper proposed a notation for conceptual information [2]; his 1966 thesis added marker passing, a way to spread activation through the net [1].
- **1975.** Woods's "What's in a Link" criticised the weak logical foundation of networks, and researchers began adding formal semantics [2][3]. See [Formal foundations](../history/formal-foundations.md).
- **1979 to 1985.** The line leads to [KL-ONE](./kl-one.md) and [description logics](./description-logic.md) [1][3]. See [Knowledge systems](../history/knowledge-systems.md).
- **2001 onward.** Sowa calls the [Semantic Web](./semantic-web.md) "a large semantic network" [1]. Hogan et al. write that semantic networks are "an early version of knowledge graphs" [3]. See [The semantic web and linked data](../history/semantic-web.md).

## Where sources disagree

**Who started it.** Gutierrez and Sequeda name Richens (1956), with Quillian developing the notion independently [2]. Sowa says Masterman's group designed "the first systems to be called semantic networks", and credits Richens with the first use of the term [1]. Hogan et al. do not say who first built semantic networks. They write that computer programs coding knowledge "can be traced back to" works by Richens (1956, spelled "Ritchens" there), Quillian (1963) and Travers and Milgram (1969) [3].

**When KL-ONE arrived.** Sowa gives Brachman (1979) as the implementation of Woods's proposal [1]; Hogan et al. date the system to Brachman and Schmolze (1985) [3].

**Whether they had meaning.** Sowa writes that some networks are "formally defined systems of logic" and others are informal [1]. Hogan et al. quote Sowa's 1987 entry: Woods and McDermott observed that the networks "have no well-defined semantics" [3].
