# Knowledge bases and knowledge graphs

How the ideas of a knowledge base and a knowledge graph evolved, from the first machines that held
knowledge in the 1950s to language models and agents that build and query it today; which approaches are
moving now; and the projects, papers, datasets and benchmarks behind them. This is the owner's research
notebook, kept public. Every statement cites a source and the time it was read.

## How it is kept

- **Settled or moving.** The History chapter holds what is settled: what became a standard, was absorbed
  into common practice, or was replaced by a successor. Everything still contested or changing is a trend.
- **Sources and times.** Every fact about a project, paper, dataset, benchmark or standard cites its source
  and the UTC time it was read. Where the sources are silent the page says **unstated**.
- **Signals, not verdicts.** Star counts carry their date. Results run by a project's own authors are
  marked first-party. An assessment is labelled as a reading.
- **Licences** are read from each project's `LICENSE` file, not from a badge.
- **Updates on request.** A new project, paper, dataset, benchmark or feature is added on request, with a
  dated row in that chapter's list of changes.
