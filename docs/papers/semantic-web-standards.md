# Papers: Semantic web standards

This group holds the papers behind [Semantic web standards](../trends/semantic-web-standards.md): the W3C languages for [RDF](../index/rdf.md) data, its query language [SPARQL](../index/sparql.md) and its validation language [SHACL](../index/shacl.md), and their revision from RDF 1.1 to RDF 1.2. The standards are not papers; they are [index](../index/README.md) entries, and the trend page tells them. Five papers are fundamental: the formal semantics of SPARQL (2006); three papers of the [RDF-star](../index/rdf-star.md) line, which became the triple terms of RDF 1.2 (two of 2014 and one of 2017); and a formal semantics of recursive SHACL (2018), a problem that SHACL 1.2 Core still leaves open. The group was added on 20261103 at the owner's review of the chapter, which until then had no group for this trend.

The standards cite none of these papers. The reference lists of the 1.2 drafts read hold W3C, IETF, Unicode and similar documents, and a text search for the five papers and their authors found nothing; RDF 1.2 Semantics lists two papers by ter Horst on RDFS entailment, which are not on this page [1]. The links run the other way. A W3C charter names Hartig and Thompson as the proposers of RDF-star in 2014 [9], the community report cites the 2017 paper [10], and SHACL 1.2 Core states a gap that the SHACL paper addresses [21]. Pérez et al. is older than every trend of the chapter; it is placed here because two of the three RDF-star papers (Hartig and Thompson, and Hartig 2017) build on it [8][11]. The third, the Reconciliation paper, does not cite it [13]. Counts are from OpenAlex unless marked. Semantic Scholar refused most requests on 20261103 with HTTP 429 (rate limit), so its counts are given only where it answered; a count is never compared with one from another source [3][4]. The papers that were checked against the four tests (origin, turning point, widely built on, survey of record) and do not pass are in Further reading, each with its reason.

## Pérez et al. (2006)

**Semantics and Complexity of SPARQL.** Jorge Pérez, Marcelo Arenas, Claudio Gutierrez (3 authors in total). arXiv cs/0605124, first version 20060526; published at ISWC 2006 (pages 30 to 43) and as an article in ACM Transactions on Database Systems 34(3), 2009 [2][3][4][8][17][29]. <https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0605124>

**Why fundamental:** *Widely built on*, and a *turning point* for formal work on SPARQL (the test asks for a later paper that extends it, and the two RDF-star papers below do). Semantic Scholar shows 1,761 citations for its record, which joins the arXiv text and the journal article (read 20261103 09:27 UTC, again 10:08) [3]; OpenAlex counts the versions apart: 1,137 for the article, 380 for the ISWC paper and 31 for the arXiv record [4]. The journal article has 45 pages and a longer abstract than the 27-page arXiv text; whether the wording is the same is **unstated** [29][2]. The SPARQL 1.0 Recommendation (20080115) lists a 2006 report "Semantics of SPARQL" by the same three authors among its informative references, without citing it in the text; the reference links to arxiv.org/abs/cs/0605124, so it is this arXiv record under another title [5]. The two RDF-star papers define SPARQL⋆ from "Pérez et al.'s algebraic syntax", citing the 2009 article [8][11]; a 2024 paper on RDF-star uses its filter logic [17]. The SPARQL 1.1 Recommendation and the SPARQL 1.2 Query draft do not name it [5][1]. *Reading:* that the W3C definition of SPARQL follows this paper is **not shown** by any source read; the 2008 Recommendation only thanks "Jorge Peérez" (sic) among people "exploring specific issues related to SPARQL" [5]. No origin is claimed. The abstract says the authors "address systematically the formal study of SPARQL", the introduction (first-party) says "To the best of our knowledge, there is no work today addressing this formalization systematically", and their related work names earlier models by Cyganiak, Harris and de Bruijn and colleagues [2]. An arXiv search made for this page found no record with SPARQL in the abstract before 20060526 [6], so this is the first arXiv record, not the first work.

**Idea:** Take a fragment of SPARQL graph patterns, without literals and with simple filters, and give it a compositional semantics. Prove normal forms and complexity bounds: evaluation of general patterns is PSPACE-complete. Compare the semantics with an alternative operational one and state conditions under which they coincide [2].

**Relations:** None.

**Read:** full text (arXiv PDF, converted to text); abstract, introduction and related work read, proofs not read. Read 20261103 09:38 UTC [2]; Semantic Scholar record 09:27 UTC [3]; SPARQL 1.0 and 1.1 Recommendations 09:38 UTC [5].

## Hartig and Thompson (2014)

**Foundations of an Alternative Approach to Reification in RDF.** Olaf Hartig, Bryan Thompson (2 authors in total). No venue: an arXiv report, versions 20140613, 20190319 and 20211216 [7]. First version 20140613. <https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.3399>

**Why fundamental:** *Origin*, as the W3C states it (narrowed below), and a *turning point*. The charter of the RDF & SPARQL Working Group says: "RDF-star (formerly spelled RDF*) is an extension of RDF that was initially proposed in 2014 by Olaf Hartig and Bryan Thompson" [9]. The charter names the authors, not this title; the arXiv record is the 2014 proposal by those two. Version 3 (20211216, by the authors) says its ideas "have been picked up and developed further by a task force" of the RDF-DEV community group, which renamed the approach RDF-star [8]; that is first-party. Independent papers take it up: Abuoda et al. (2022) cite it, with Hartig's reconciliation paper below, as the proposal of RDF-star [14], and Hogan et al. cite it for graph models whose nodes contain edges [16]. It has 40 citations (OpenAlex, read 20261103 09:28 UTC); the Semantic Scholar request returned HTTP 429, so that count is **unstated** [4][3]. Hartig is the first editor of SPARQL 1.2 Query and an editor of RDF 1.2 Concepts [1].

*Reading:* the origin is narrower than the charter's sentence. The paper says that several participants of a Dagstuhl seminar of 2012, both authors among them, discussed the approach; it has no related-work section and cites seven references [8]. The seminar's report (Dagstuhl Reports 2(4), published 20120921) holds Thompson's abstract "Reification made easy": an indexing scheme for RDF that can "answer queries about both ground statements and metadata about those statements". A group formed at the seminar (Hartig, Tran, Erling and Rankka, Thompson and Personick) "has formalized the model theory for the proposed approach and reconciled it with the RDF model theory and the SPARQL algebra", aiming at a W3C proposal [27]. So a proposal for statements about statements, a group around it and the aim of standardisation are dated 2012 (first-party), two years before the arXiv report, which carries two of the six names. The abstract does not describe nested triples, so whether it is the same design is **unstated**. Singleton Property, another compact alternative to reification, was published at WWW in April 2014, two months before the report (Further reading) [18]. An arXiv search on reification and statement-level metadata found no earlier record; one on provenance and quads found annotated RDF with a SPARQL-inspired language (Zimmermann et al., arXiv 1103.1255, 20110307; abstract only, no nesting) [12]. What the report adds is the nesting itself as a formal data model: a triple in the subject or object position of another, with a query language and a mapping to ordinary RDF. The count is small; one possible reason is that the line reached the standard through the group's report and drafts, which cite few papers [1][10]. That is a guess, not a measured cause.

**Idea:** A triple may stand as the subject or object of another triple, so metadata about a statement is written as a statement about it, instead of four reification triples. The paper defines this data model (RDF⋆), a Turtle syntax (Turtle⋆), a query language (SPARQL⋆) with nested triple patterns, and an extension of the SPARQL 1.1 grammar and algebra translation. A mapping to plain RDF through the reification vocabulary keeps it backwards compatible. The names RDF⋆, Turtle⋆ and SPARQL⋆ are already in version 1 [8].

**Relations:** Builds on: [Pérez et al. (2006)](./semantic-web-standards.md), through the 2009 article it cites for the algebraic syntax of SPARQL on which SPARQL⋆ is defined [8].

**Read:** full text (arXiv PDF, versions 1 and 3, converted to text): pages 1 to 7 and 13 to 15 read, the definitions of pages 8 to 12 skimmed. Read 20261103 09:24 UTC; arXiv record 09:24 and 09:27 UTC [7][8].

## Hartig Reconciliation (2014)

**Reconciliation of RDF⋆ and Property Graphs.** Olaf Hartig (1 author). No venue: an arXiv report, versions 20140911 and 20141113 [13]. First version 20140911. <https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.3288>

**Why fundamental:** *Turning point*, in a narrow sense. The working group's charter calls RDF-star popular "especially as a means to provide interoperability between RDF and Property Graphs" [9]. Two papers by other authors take this report up (they share one author, Katja Hose). Abuoda et al. (Aalborg University and ArangoDB, 2022) describe its two mappings from RDF-star to a [property graph](../index/property-graph.md) and run the first with the implementation that goes with it: "Our analysis (Section 6) includes this approach using the authors' implementation" [14]. Jakubowski et al. (20261024, on RDF 1.2) cite it as the work that formalises the property graph model and defines transformations in both directions; two of their seven authors, Tomaszuk and Taelman, edit RDF 1.2 and SPARQL 1.2 drafts [15][1]. It has 54 citations (OpenAlex, read 20261103 09:29 UTC); the Semantic Scholar count is **unstated** (HTTP 429) [4]. No origin is claimed: Jakubowski et al. describe an earlier mapping of property graphs to RDF, by Das et al. in Oracle (EDBT 2014) [15], and the only arXiv record found before 20140911 with property graphs and RDF in the abstract is the Hartig and Thompson report [12].

**Idea:** Give the property graph model, which had no agreed formal definition, a formalisation, and define transformations in both directions between property graphs and RDF. RDF⋆ serves as the basis of the reconciliation [13]; in the direction from RDF-star to a property graph, the metadata triples about a triple become the properties of an edge [14].

**Relations:** Builds on: [Hartig and Thompson (2014)](./semantic-web-standards.md), which it cites for RDF⋆ and Turtle⋆ [13].

**Read:** arXiv PDF (versions 1 and 2, converted to text), 18 pages: the abstract, the introduction and the start of the informal overview (pages 1 to 3) read; the rest, including the formal sections, searched only. Read 20261103 09:36 to 09:39 UTC [13].

## Hartig (2017)

**Foundations of RDF⋆ and SPARQL⋆ (An Alternative Approach to Statement-Level Metadata in RDF).** Olaf Hartig (1 author). 11th Alberto Mendelzon International Workshop on Foundations of Data Management and the Web (AMW), Montevideo, 7 to 9 June 2017; CEUR Workshop Proceedings, Vol. 1912, paper 12, published 20170829 [11]. First version 20170829. <http://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1912/paper12.pdf>

**Why fundamental:** *Turning point*. The Final Community Group Report on RDF-star and SPARQL-star (20211217) lists this paper, not the arXiv report, as its reference RDF-STAR-FOUNDATION; it cites it for the verbosity of reification and, in an appendix, speaks of the motivating example in "the original RDF-star paper" [10]. The trend page tells how that report led to the triple terms of RDF 1.2. Later papers cite it as the source of the idea: Hogan et al. for RDF* [16]; Rupp et al. (2022) [17]; and Jakubowski et al. (20261024), who open with "a long line of work on statement-level metadata, most prominently RDF-star" and give this paper as the reference [15]. It has 50 citations (OpenAlex, read 20261103 09:29 UTC; the record is the workshop's copy at KTH) [4]; the Semantic Scholar count is **unstated** [3]. Hartig's four-page ISWC poster of the same year is cited by the RDF-star embedding papers instead (Further reading) [17]. No origin is claimed: it proposes the nesting of triples that the 2014 report already defined, and cites that report as "our technical report" [11].

**Idea:** "We propose an alternative approach that is based on nesting of RDF triples and of query patterns" [11]. The paper defines RDF⋆ and the semantics of SPARQL⋆ formally. It gives two desirable properties of mappings to ordinary RDF and SPARQL, information preservation and query result equivalence, and proves that mappings built on the reification vocabulary have them. So the approach can be a wrapper on top of any triple store, or be implemented natively. It names the shortcomings of reification, singleton properties and named graphs as its motive, each of which still needs verbose queries [11].

**Relations:** Builds on: [Hartig and Thompson (2014)](./semantic-web-standards.md), which it cites as "our technical report", and [Pérez et al. (2006)](./semantic-web-standards.md), through the 2009 article it cites (reference 7) [11]. Answers: also: Singleton Property (Nguyen et al. 2014) and the named-graph approach of Hernández et al. (2015), both in Further reading; the introduction says each "still requires verbose constructs in queries" [11].

**Read:** CEUR PDF (converted to text), eleven pages: the abstract, the introduction, section 2 up to the syntax of SPARQL⋆, the statement of Theorem 2, the outlook and the references read; the semantics, the mappings and the proofs (sections 2.3 to 4) searched only. Read 20261103 09:26 UTC; the proceedings page 09:27 UTC [11].

## Corman et al. (2018)

**Semantics and Validation of Recursive SHACL.** Julien Corman, Juan L. Reutter, Ognjen Savković (3 authors in total). The Semantic Web, ISWC 2018 (Lecture Notes in Computer Science 11136), pages 318 to 336, online 20180918 [4][28]. Earlier dated sources: a four-page extended abstract by the same authors, "Towards a Robust Semantics for SHACL: Preliminary Discussion", in the proceedings of the AMW workshop (CEUR-WS Vol. 2100, published 20180520), and the message of 20180618 in which a member of the W3C SHACL list points to the paper; the extended report is dated 20181106 [28][19][20]. <https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-00671-6_19>

**Why fundamental:** *Turning point*, in the research line only, and weak. The Recommendation of 2017 leaves open how to validate against recursive shapes; the paper gives a semantics for the core constraint components, with partial assignments of shapes to nodes [19]. Bogaerts and Jakubowski (2021; two authors, neither a co-author) take its three-valued operator as a starting point for a family of semantics and show that theirs and the paper's supported-model semantics "coincide" [22]. Hogan et al. cite it for the semantics of recursion by partial assignments [16]. Ahmetaj et al. (20260422) write that "The challenges of recursion were first formally addressed by Corman et al."; one of its twelve authors, Savković, is a co-author, so this is partly first-party [22]. On 20180618 Peter F. Patel-Schneider, a name that also appears among the editors of RDF 1.2 Semantics, pointed the W3C SHACL list to the paper [20][1]. Citations: 97 (Semantic Scholar, read 20261103 09:44 UTC) and 72 (OpenAlex, read 09:34 UTC), two sources not compared [3][4]; Crossref (48) and the publisher's page (69) give two more numbers for the same chapter (read 10:14 and 10:15 UTC), which is why counts from different sources are not compared [28].

*Reading:* the standard has not taken it up. SHACL 1.2 Core (Working Draft 20261102, section 6.7.3) still says "The validation with recursive shapes is not defined in SHACL and is left to SHACL processor implementations", and expects future work in Community Groups to define dialects with recursion [21]. The Data Shapes charter says SHACL 1.2 Core "should fix some unfinished issues in SHACL 1.0, such as recursion" [21]. A tool and test suite of 2026 (sheval) finds SHACL engines behaving differently on recursive shapes [22]. Ognjen Savković is thanked among the contributors to SPARQL 1.2 RL, written in the Data Shapes group's rules task force [1]. The origin is narrowed: an earlier arXiv record, Ryman's "Recursion in RDF Data Shape Languages" (20150519), defines what recursion means in shape languages, as used in Resource Shape 2.0, for the W3C working group; and ShEx supported recursion from its first version, without negation [23][19]. Formal semantics for ShEx schemas were on arXiv from 20140404 (Boneva et al., arXiv 1404.1270, first titled "Validating RDF with Shape Expressions"; abstracts of versions 1 and 3 read). Version 3 of 20170919, retitled "Semantics and Validation of Shapes Schemas for RDF" (the ISWC 2017 paper), allows recursive references with stratified use of negation and recursion [23]. The extended report says that semantics "is restricted to stratified constraints" (related work) [19]. What the paper adds, by its own claim (first-party), is a semantics for arbitrary recursion in SHACL [19].

**Idea:** A graph is valid if some assignment of shapes to nodes verifies both the constraints and the targets. The assignment may be partial, because a constraint's truth value at a node can be unknown. The authors show that validation against recursive SHACL is NP-hard in the size of the graph, even with stratified negation, and give a tractable approximation [19].

**Relations:** None.

**Read:** full text of the extended technical report (KRDB 18-01, 43 pages, converted to text). The proceedings version (16 pages; same title and abstract; the report adds an appendix of proofs and a translation from SHACL to the abstract syntax) was checked by a second reader for the abstract, the quoted sentence of the SHACL Recommendation, the NP-hardness claim, partial assignments and the ShEx related work, which agree with the report [28]. Read 20261103 09:37 UTC; the W3C list message 09:37 UTC; the proceedings version 10:14 to 10:30 UTC [19][20][28].
