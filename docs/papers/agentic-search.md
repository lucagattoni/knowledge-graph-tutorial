# Papers: Agentic search over raw data

This group holds the fundamental papers behind the trend [Agentic search over raw data](../trends/agentic-search.md), in which an [agent](../index/agent.md) searches unmodified files, logs or passages itself instead of querying a graph, tree or memory store built beforehand. Five papers from 2022 to 2024 set up the loop and the question of how much to retrieve or read beyond one fixed [retrieval-augmented generation](../index/retrieval-augmented-generation.md) step; two from 2026 that test raw search directly against built structure, Is Grep All You Need? and ReFind, are in the further reading: each result is first-party and unreplicated, and no later paper read uses either as a baseline or extends it. No paper started the line of work, and no section below is labelled "origin": no source read shows that any of these papers came first. Its clearest non-paper statement is Anthropic's engineering post of 20250929, which says Claude Code uses glob and grep to "retrieve files just-in-time, effectively bypassing the issues of stale indexing and complex syntax trees" [14]; whether that post is the origin is unstated.

## ReAct (2022)

**Paper:** "ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models", Shunyu Yao, Jeffrey Zhao, Dian Yu and 4 others (7 authors in total); ICLR 2023 (the arXiv comment calls v3 the ICLR camera-ready version; the year is from the dblp key in the Semantic Scholar record); first version 20221006; <https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629> [1][8].

**Why fundamental:** Widely built on. It has 13,331 citations in Semantic Scholar (read 20261102 20:20 UTC, carried over) [8]. IRCoT's related work discusses it [25], and two papers in the further reading point back to it: Is Grep All You Need? calls it "the most widely adopted pattern for custom harnesses" [12] and ReFind runs "a ReAct-style agent loop" [13]. A [large language model](../index/large-language-model.md) writes reasoning and task actions in one interleaved trace, with actions that "interface with external sources, such as knowledge bases or environments" [1]. The abstract says reasoning and acting had "primarily been studied as separate topics" [1] (the authors' own framing); whether an earlier paper first interleaved reasoning with search is unstated, so this page does not call it an origin.

**Idea:** the model is prompted to alternate "thought" and "action" steps. The reasoning "help[s] the model induce, track, and update action plans as well as handle exceptions", and the action, such as a query to a Wikipedia API, brings back the evidence for the next thought [1]. On [HotpotQA](../datasets/hotpotqa.md) and Fever this reduces the hallucination and error propagation of [chain-of-thought](../index/chain-of-thought.md) alone, and on ALFWorld and WebShop it beats imitation and reinforcement-learning methods by 34 and 10 points absolute with one or two examples in the prompt [1]. These numbers are the authors' own (first-party). It builds on chain-of-thought prompting, which the abstract names (not a paper of this chapter) [1]. It is scored on [HotpotQA](../datasets/hotpotqa.md) ([benchmark page](../benchmarks/hotpotqa.md)) [1].

**Relations:** None.

**Read:** abstract and record on the arXiv page, 20261102 20:41 UTC [1]; abstract and first-version date read again through the arXiv API, 20261102 20:56 UTC [24]; the full text was not read.

## IRCoT (2022)

**Paper:** "Interleaving Retrieval with Chain-of-Thought Reasoning for Knowledge-Intensive Multi-Step Questions", Harsh Trivedi, Niranjan Balasubramanian, Tushar Khot and 1 other (4 authors in total); ACL 2023 (arXiv comment "ACL'23 Camera Ready"); first version 20221220; <https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10509> [2].

**Why fundamental:** Widely built on. It has 1,479 citations in Semantic Scholar (read 20261102 20:20 UTC, carried over) [8], and Is Grep All You Need? (further reading) cites it, with Self-RAG and FLARE, as agentic search [12]. It is not called an origin: ReAct is earlier (first versions 20221006 and 20221220) and already pairs reasoning with a Wikipedia API [24][1]. What IRCoT adds is narrower: the reasoning so far steers each retrieval, because "what to retrieve depends on what has already been derived" [2]. Its related work calls ReAct's steps "much more complex", dependent on much larger models and on fine-tuning [25] (first-party view of a competitor). Its place here is the problem it states for [multi-hop questions](../index/multi-hop-question-answering.md): one retrieval step before reading is not enough.

**Idea:** the model writes one sentence of a chain of thought, that sentence becomes the query for the next retrieval, and the retrieved paragraphs feed the next sentence [2]. It needs no extra training. The authors report up to 21 points better retrieval and up to 15 points better answers on four datasets, with GPT3 and with Flan-T5-large [2] (first-party). It answers the one-step retrieve-and-read setting, which its abstract calls "insufficient for multi-step QA" [2]; that is the setting of [Lewis et al. (2020)](./foundations.md), which the abstract does not name. It builds on chain-of-thought prompting (not a paper of this chapter) [2] and compares itself with [ReAct (2022)](./agentic-search.md) in its related work, as above [25]. It is scored on [HotpotQA](../datasets/hotpotqa.md) ([benchmark page](../benchmarks/hotpotqa.md)), [2WikiMultihopQA](../datasets/2wikimultihopqa.md) ([benchmark page](../benchmarks/2wikimultihopqa.md)) and [MuSiQue](../datasets/musique.md) ([benchmark page](../benchmarks/musique.md)), and IIRC, which has no page here [2].

**Relations:** None.

**Read:** abstract and record on the arXiv page, 20261102 20:41 UTC [2]; the arXiv HTML full text was searched for its mentions of ReAct and its related work, 20261102 20:56 UTC [25].

## Lost in the Middle (2023)

**Paper:** "Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts", Nelson F. Liu, Kevin Lin, John Hewitt and 4 others (7 authors in total); Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics, 2023 (arXiv comment: accepted); first version 20230706; <https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172> [3].

**Why fundamental:** Turning point and widely built on. It showed that putting more text into a [context window](../index/context-window.md) does not mean the model uses it: performance "can degrade significantly when changing the position of relevant information", and is lowest in the middle "even for explicitly long-context models" [3]. That moved the argument from how large a window is to how it is used. A later comparison of retrieval and long context cites it [10], and Is Grep All You Need? (further reading) cites it for the pressure that large tool results put on the window [12]. It has 5,799 citations in Semantic Scholar (read 20261102 20:20 UTC, carried over) [8].

**Idea:** two tasks probe a model's use of its input. In multi-document question answering the number of documents and the position of the one that holds the answer are varied; the second task is key-value retrieval [3][9]. Accuracy is highest when the relevant item is at the start or the end. In the open-domain setting, "model performance saturates long before retriever recall saturates", so adding retrieved documents stops helping [9]. It is scored on [NaturalQuestions](../datasets/natural-questions.md) ([benchmark page](../benchmarks/natural-questions.md)): its question-answering data are built from NaturalQuestions-Open with retrieved distractor documents [9]. The task, in its words, "mimics the retrieval-augmented generation setup" of generative search [9]. A search of its full text finds no citation of the RAG paper of 2020 [9].

**Relations:** None.

**Read:** abstract on the arXiv page, 20261102 20:41 UTC [3]; the arXiv HTML full text was searched for the dataset and the retrieval setting, 20261102 20:43 UTC [9].

## Self-RAG (2023)

**Paper:** "Self-RAG: Learning to Retrieve, Generate, and Critique through Self-Reflection", Akari Asai, Zeqiu Wu, Yizhong Wang and 2 others (5 authors in total); the arXiv record names no venue (Semantic Scholar lists ICLR, and the 2026 paper cites it as 2024); first version 20231017; <https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511> [4][8][12].

**Why fundamental:** Widely built on. It has 3,006 citations in Semantic Scholar (read 20261102 20:20 UTC, carried over) [8], and Is Grep All You Need? (further reading) cites it twice as agentic search, with FLARE and either IRCoT or ReAct [12]. It is not called an origin: FLARE (further reading) was first posted 20230511, five months earlier, and covers methods that "actively decide when and what to retrieve" [24]; Self-RAG's own related work cites it [11]. What Self-RAG adds is training: one model that "adaptively retrieves passages on-demand" and judges what it retrieves, instead of always retrieving a fixed number of passages [4].

**Idea:** special reflection tokens let a single model say whether retrieval is needed, whether a passage is relevant, and whether its own output is supported [4]. Because the tokens are generated, a user can steer the model at inference time. The authors report that 7B and 13B versions beat ChatGPT and retrieval-augmented Llama2-chat on open-domain question answering, reasoning and fact verification [4] (first-party). FLARE instead triggers retrieval when the upcoming sentence has low-confidence tokens [24]. It answers [Lewis et al. (2020)](./foundations.md): the abstract objects to "indiscriminately retrieving and incorporating a fixed number of retrieved passages" [4]. Its introduction cites Lewis et al. (2020) and [Guu et al. (2020)](./foundations.md) as the retrieval-augmented methods, and its related work cites FLARE (further reading) as earlier adaptive retrieval [11]. It is scored on [PopQA](../datasets/popqa.md) and [TriviaQA](../datasets/triviaqa.md) [11].

**Relations:** Builds on: [Guu et al. (2020)](./foundations.md), [Lewis et al. (2020)](./foundations.md). Answers: [Lewis et al. (2020)](./foundations.md).

**Read:** abstract on the arXiv page, 20261102 20:41 UTC [4]; the arXiv HTML full text was searched for related work and datasets, 20261102 20:43 UTC, and for its citation of Jiang et al. (2023), 20:56 UTC [11]; FLARE's abstract through the arXiv API, 20:56 UTC [24].

## RAG or Long-Context LLMs (2024)

**Paper:** "Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs? A Comprehensive Study and Hybrid Approach", Zhuowan Li, Cheng Li, Mingyang Zhang and 2 others (5 authors in total); EMNLP 2024, industry track; first version 20240723; <https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16833> [5].

**Why fundamental:** Turning point. It frames the choice between retrieving and reading everything as a routing decision per query. With three recent models it finds that "when resourced sufficiently, LC consistently outperforms RAG" on average, while RAG's "significantly lower cost remains a distinct advantage" [5]. A later paper builds its case on that finding: Cache-Augmented Generation (further reading, 20241220) cites it, among studies that "investigated the performance of long-context models in RAG tasks", in the argument for preloading the corpus instead of retrieving [26]. It has 199 citations in Semantic Scholar (read 20261102 20:20 UTC, carried over), so the test rests on the finding and that citing paper, not on a count [8]. *Reading:* the cost-against-accuracy trade-off it measures for plain retrieval is the one the 2026 papers of this group's further reading measure for agents that search.

**Idea:** the study benchmarks retrieval-augmented generation against long-context models on public datasets. Self-Route, the method it proposes, routes each query to retrieval or to the long-context path by the model's own self-reflection, and so keeps close to long-context accuracy at lower cost [5]. The authors' own analysis includes why retrieval fails and the effect of different retrievers [10]. It answers Retrieval meets Long Context (2023), listed in [Further reading](./agentic-search.md) below: "Different from findings in previous work Xu et al. (2023), we find that LC consistently outperforms RAG" [10]. Its related work cites [Lost in the Middle (2023)](./agentic-search.md) [10]. It is scored on subsets of LongBench and InfiniteBench, which have no page here [10].

**Relations:** Answers: also: Retrieval meets Long Context (2023).

**Read:** abstract on the arXiv page, 20261102 20:41 UTC [5]; the arXiv HTML full text was searched for related work and datasets, 20261102 20:43 UTC [10].

## Further reading

- FLARE, "Active Retrieval Augmented Generation" (2023): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06983>; Zhengbao Jiang and 8 others (9 authors in total); EMNLP 2023 per the arXiv comment; first version 20230511; retrieval is triggered during generation when the upcoming sentence has low-confidence tokens; earlier than Self-RAG, which cites it; 950 citations in Semantic Scholar (read 20261102 20:20 UTC, carried over) [24][11][8].
- Retrieval meets Long Context (2023): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03025>; an earlier controlled comparison of retrieval against longer windows (ICLR 2024 per the arXiv comment), answered by RAG or Long-Context LLMs [15][10].
- Cache-Augmented Generation (2024): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.15605>; preloads a small corpus into the long context and skips retrieval altogether (WWW 2025 short paper per the arXiv comment) [16].
- Agentic RAG survey (2025): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09136>; "Agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation: A Survey on Agentic RAG", Singh et al., 5 authors; the survey the trend page names; 470 citations in Semantic Scholar (read 20261102 20:20 UTC) [17][8].
- Search-R1 (2025): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09516>; trains a model with reinforcement learning to use a search engine while reasoning; held in [Agentic graph retrieval](./learned-graph-traversal.md) [20].
- Direct corpus interaction (2026): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05242>; "Beyond Semantic Similarity: Rethinking Retrieval for Agentic Search via Direct Corpus Interaction", argues that a fixed top-k similarity interface is a bottleneck for agentic search [18].
- Pi-Serini (2026): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10848>; asks whether a BM25 retriever is sufficient once an agent loop and a strong model sit on top [19].
- Naive RAG Is Stronger Than We Thought (2026): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30755>; "Is Complex Agentic Memory Always Needed? Naive RAG Is Stronger Than We Thought", dense retrieval and reranking over raw history as a baseline for agent memory; listed in the further reading of [Agent memory](./agent-memory.md) [21].
- BM25 Wins at Scale (2026): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26497>; a scaling study over 28 nested corpus sizes of lexical, dense, graph-based and agentic retrieval; listed in the further reading of [Graph-based RAG](./graph-based-rag.md) [23].
- RAGFlow-team harness study (2026): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41172>; "Context as Infrastructure: Knowledge Compilation for Retrieval in Agentic Systems with Harnesses", reports a BM25-and-grep harness answering 85% of BrowseComp-Plus in the authors' runs (first-party); see [RAGFlow](../projects/ragflow.md) [22].
- Is Grep All You Need? (2026): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15184>; "Is Grep All You Need? How Agent Harnesses Reshape Agentic Search", Sahil Sen, Akhil Kasturi, Elias Lumer and 2 others (5 authors in total); first version 20260514. It compares [grep](../index/grep.md) with [vector search](../index/vector-search.md) inside three command-line [agent harnesses](../index/agent-harness.md) (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI) and the authors' own, Chronos, on 116 [LongMemEval](../datasets/longmemeval.md) questions; with inline results grep was above vector search for every harness and model pair, and the same Claude Opus 4.6 scored 93.1% in Chronos and 76.7% in Claude Code, so "changing the harness shifts the ceiling by roughly as much as swapping retrievers within a fixed harness" [6][12]. First-party and unreplicated (Chronos is by two of the same authors); the authors "do not claim that grep 'beats' vector in general" [12]. 24 citations (Semantic Scholar, read 20261102 20:20 UTC) [8]. Moved from the fundamental list: the only later paper read that cites it is the RAGFlow-team study below, as having "reached a similar conclusion", which is corroboration, not use as a baseline or an extension [27].
- ReFind (2026): <https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12888>; "When Your Agent Opens the Chat App: Agent-Controlled Search over Raw Chat Logs Rivals Structured Memory", Ruizhe Li, Licheng Zhang, Benfeng Xu and 3 others (6 authors in total); first version 20260813. A [ReAct](./agentic-search.md)-style loop with a [BM25](../index/bm25.md) search tool and four chat-specific controls answers from collected evidence; on about 2,800 questions of [MemoryAgentBench](../benchmarks/memoryagentbench.md) with GPT-4o-mini it reaches a mean accuracy of 58.2, against 53.2 for HippoRAG 2, 29.9 for GraphRAG and 27.0 for RAPTOR (scores reused from the benchmark's paper) and 48.8 for single-shot BM25 [7][13]. First-party; its citation count is unstated. Moved from the fundamental list: the only later paper read that mentions it is the Naive RAG study, as "concurrent work" in its related work [27]; the closest independent evidence is Raw-RAG, which uses no agent loop [21].
