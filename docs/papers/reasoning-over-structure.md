# Papers: Vectorless retrieval

This page holds the papers behind [the trend](../trends/reasoning-over-structure.md) in which a [language model](../index/large-language-model.md) reads a tree, or a structure it builds for the question, instead of ranking chunks by [embedding](../index/embedding.md) similarity. The line of work starts with a software project, not a paper: [PageIndex](../projects/pageindex.md), a repository created 20250401 whose README asks to be cited as a blog post of September 2025 [1]. No paper by its authors was found in the sources read. The two fundamental papers are the tree ancestor that still uses embeddings and a head-to-head against vector search that its authors call the first. LogicRAG, a method that builds its structure at query time, is in the further reading: the origin it claims does not hold up against earlier work, and no test could be shown for it.

## RAPTOR (2024)

**RAPTOR: Recursive Abstractive Processing for Tree-Organized Retrieval.** Parth Sarthi, Salman Abdullah, Aditi Tuli (6 in total). Venue: none named on the arXiv record [2]; Semantic Scholar lists the International Conference on Learning Representations, second-hand and not checked against the proceedings [10]. First version 20240131. https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18059

**Why fundamental:** Widely built on, with an origin that is contested. 840 citations, Semantic Scholar, read 20261102 20:20 UTC [10]. Three comparison studies by other authors use it as the tree baseline: [RAG vs GraphRAG](./graph-based-rag.md) and [GraphRAG-Bench Xiang](./graph-based-rag.md), both held in Graph-based RAG, and RAGSearch (further reading there) [16][17][18]; LogicRAG (further reading here) also lists it among its baselines [5]. It is the earliest tree-organised retrieval method in this group, and later papers name it as prior art [5][9], but no origin is claimed for the tree of summaries: an arXiv search made for this page (records before 20240131; source 19) returned MemWalker (20231008), whose abstract describes a method that processes a long context "into a tree of summary nodes" and has the model navigate it when it receives a query [19]. It is an ancestor, not a vectorless method: in the main experiments every node is embedded with SBERT and searched by cosine similarity [3].

**Idea:** Cut the corpus into 100-token [chunks](../index/chunking.md), embed them, cluster them with Gaussian mixture models, have a language model summarise each cluster, and repeat on the summaries until a tree stands [3]. At query time the chosen variant (the "collapsed tree") puts all nodes in one layer and takes the most similar up to 2,000 tokens, so detail and theme summaries can arrive together [3]. First-party result: with GPT-4, 20% absolute accuracy gain over the best earlier QuALITY result [2]. The tree is added on top of SBERT, BM25 or DPR retrievers and improved each of them [3]. Its reference list holds Okapi at TREC-3 ([Robertson et al. (1994)](./foundations.md)) and the monograph ([Robertson and Zaragoza (2009)](./foundations.md)) for BM25 [20], and DPR ([Karpukhin et al. (2020)](./foundations.md)) is one of its retrievers [3]. It was tested on QuALITY, NarrativeQA and QASPER, which have no page in the datasets chapter [3].

**Relations:** Builds on: [Robertson et al. (1994)](./foundations.md), [Robertson and Zaragoza (2009)](./foundations.md), [Karpukhin et al. (2020)](./foundations.md).

**Read:** full text (arXiv HTML), method and experiments; appendices searched only. Read 20261102 20:41 UTC, method and retriever comparison read again 20:56 UTC; metadata 20:40 UTC [2][3].

## Lumer et al. (2025)

**Rethinking Retrieval: From Traditional Retrieval Augmented Generation to Agentic and Non-Vector Reasoning Systems in the Financial Domain for Large Language Models.** Elias Lumer, Matt Melich, Olivia Zino (9 in total). Venue: none named; the arXiv comment says "8 pages, 2 figures" [6]. First version 20251122. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18177

**Why fundamental:** One test, in its weak and provisional form: *turning point (independent evaluation)*. The evidence is a later paper by other authors: Adaptive Query Routing (2026, one author, further reading) says it "extends their work" and re-tests the same two designs on a small controlled corpus [14]. The authors of the paper itself give PricewaterhouseCoopers U.S. as their affiliation, and they call theirs "the first systematic evaluation" of the two designs [6][7]; the later paper repeats that wording [14]. The citation count is unstated (not in the citation pools; the APIs were not queried again). *Reading:* the priority is the authors' own claim, not a checked first. Limits: one study; their own node-tree implementation, which cites PageIndex as its basis; an architecture comparison on 75 questions, judged pairwise by a language model (Claude 4.5 Sonnet) [7].

**Idea:** Two systems are compared over a corpus of 1,200 SEC filings. One is agentic RAG with 512-token chunks, embeddings, hybrid search and metadata filters. The other builds a table-of-contents node tree per filing with GPT-4o; the model picks nodes and reads their pages, with no embeddings [7]. The architecture comparison used a 75-question subset of the authors' 150-question benchmark; the other two subsets of 50 questions test reranking and small-to-big retrieval [7]. Vector agentic RAG won 68% of pairwise comparisons at similar latency (5.2 against 5.98 seconds); the tree system failed 2 questions and answered 2 wrongly, which the authors trace to context limits and to node selection, and they propose vector search to narrow the candidates [7]. The abstract and conclusion state the 68% against the full 150-question benchmark; the body ties it to the subset [6][7]. It builds on [Lost in the Middle (2023)](./agentic-search.md) for the context-window limit and on [Gao et al. (2023)](./foundations.md) for its pre-, intra- and post-retrieval split of RAG methods [7]. It evaluates a node-tree system built on the approach of [PageIndex](../projects/pageindex.md) (a project with no paper) against vector agentic RAG, on its own 150-question benchmark; it cites FinanceBench, which has no benchmark page [7].

**Relations:** Builds on: [Lost in the Middle (2023)](./agentic-search.md), [Gao et al. (2023)](./foundations.md). Evaluates: also: PageIndex (a project, no paper).

**Read:** full text (arXiv HTML), all sections. Read 20261102 20:54 UTC; metadata 20:54 UTC [6][7].

## Further reading

- LogicRAG, 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06105 : "You Don't Need Pre-built Graphs for RAG: Retrieval Augmented Generation with Adaptive Reasoning Structures" (AAAI 2026); a model splits the question into sub-problems, links them in a directed acyclic graph by logical dependency, orders them by topological sort and retrieves for each in turn; first-party, 64.7% string accuracy on 2WikiMultiHopQA, 14.7 points above HippoRAG 2; 41 citations (Semantic Scholar, read 20261102 20:20 UTC); retrieval still uses an embedding model [4][5][10]. Moved from the fundamental list: it states the choice of a structure made per question, but earlier records do the same at inference time, among them StructRAG (arXiv 2410.08815, 20241011, which "can identify the optimal structure type for the task at hand" and reconstructs documents into it) and KnowTrace (2505.20245, 20250526, which traces a question-specific knowledge graph during iterative retrieval) [19]; and no paper in the sources read for this page builds on it.
- MemWalker, 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05029 : "Walking Down the Memory Maze: Beyond Context Limit through Interactive Reading"; the long context becomes a tree of summary nodes that the model navigates in search of the answer, three and a half months before RAPTOR and closer to a model reasoning over a tree than RAPTOR's embedding search (v1 20231008) [19].
- RIT-RAG, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11370 : a tree per document from its table of contents; retrieval picks chunks and their positions induce the sub-trees an agent walks, answering the scale limit of a single-document tree (v1 20261008) [11].
- DecisionRAG, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35868 : "Retriever-Free Retrieval"; a decision model judges all branches in one forward pass, with no tokens generated (v1 20261026) [12].
- DeepRead, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05014 : OCR structure becomes a paragraph-level navigation system with Retrieve and ReadSection tools (v1 20260204; 10 citations, Semantic Scholar, read 20261102 20:20 UTC, carried over; abstract only) [13][10].
- Adaptive Query Routing, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14222 : one author, a small corpus, extending Lumer et al.; tree reasoning scored highest overall (0.900) but no paradigm won every query tier (v1 20260414, v2 20261028) [14].
- KITE, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41521 : a vector-free conversational memory; a boundary case that also belongs to agent memory (v1 20261030) [15].
- Context as Infrastructure, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41172 : the [RAGFlow](../projects/ragflow.md) team's study, which is not listed as fundamental because it passes no test yet: it is first-party for RAGFlow, three days old at the read, has no citation count, and tests a PageIndex-style outline RAGFlow built itself, which it found neutral at small scale and harmful at FRAMES scale, with no structural artifact adding a detectable accuracy gain to a BM25-and-grep harness on [FRAMES](../benchmarks/frames.md) (v1 20261030) [8][9].
