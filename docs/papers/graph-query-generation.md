# Papers: Asking graphs in natural language

This group holds the papers behind [Asking graphs in natural language](../trends/graph-query-generation.md): a [language model](../index/large-language-model.md) turns a question into a [Cypher](../index/cypher.md) or [SPARQL](../index/sparql.md) query, and the [graph database](../index/graph-database.md) runs it ([text-to-query](../index/text-to-query.md)). One paper is listed as fundamental, Text2Cypher (2024). Its main contribution is a dataset, a benchmark run and a fine-tuning study, so it is told here for one reason only: other papers build on it (the test "widely built on"), on a small count. Its data and protocol are told in the [Text2Cypher benchmark](../benchmarks/text2cypher.md) page. The SPARQL side has no fundamental paper. No other paper in the candidate pools passed a test, so the group stays at one paper: SPARQL-LLM extends its authors' earlier system (the earlier paper, of 20241008, is in the arXiv records [15]) and has no count and no later citation among the papers read; Text2Cypher Across Languages translates the questions of Text2Cypher and has 3 citations; Text2GraphQuery-Bench is a benchmark paper with 2 citations, whose data belongs to the datasets and benchmarks chapters (see Further reading). No blog post or gist is named as the origin of this line in the sources read; the Text2Cypher paper cites a Neo4j Labs crowdsourcing post and a Neo4j developer-blog post as sources of data and prompt tips, and whether either started the line is **unstated** [2].

## Text2Cypher (2024)

*Text2Cypher: Bridging Natural Language and Graph Databases.* Makbule Gulcin Ozsoy, Leila Messallem, Jon Besga (4 authors in total) [1]. GenAIK workshop (Generative AI and Knowledge Graphs), Abu Dhabi, January 2025 [3]. First version 20241213 [1]. <https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10064>

**Why fundamental:** One test, *widely built on*, passed on a small count. Later papers use its data or cite it: LAST-CQ uses its benchmark, and Text2GraphQuery-Bench and Across Languages use its data [5][6][7][8][9][10]. Semantic Scholar shows 73 citations (read 20261102 19:47 UTC) [4]. It is listed although a dataset paper normally lives in the datasets and benchmarks chapters, because it is the only paper of this line that others build on. The paper does not claim to start the task, and its related work lists earlier Cypher datasets and fine-tuned models that "are prepared independently", the gap it fills [2]; an arXiv search made for this page (source 15) also returned earlier text-to-Cypher work, among it SyntheT2C (20240615), so no origin is claimed.

*Reading:* the test is passed on weak evidence. The count is modest. The six Cypher-side papers read here all cite it or use its data, but the six are the trend's own list, not a sample, and two of them share its first author, Ozsoy (Across Languages, by two authors, and CypherGuard, by four) [5][8]. All four authors give Neo4j as their affiliation [2], so its scores are first-party.

**Idea:** merge the public question-and-Cypher pairs into one cleaned dataset with fixed splits, then score models on it and fine-tune some. The authors found 25 resources, used 16, and checked every query with `EXPLAIN`, ending with 44,387 instances (89% training, 11% test) [2]. They scored 14 models on Google-BLEU (query text) and Exact Match (executed results). The conclusion states that the fine-tuned models beat their baselines, "up to a 0.34 increase in Google-BLEU score and a 0.11 increase in Exact Match score" [2]. They name their own risks, among them paraphrases that may inflate scores [2]. It answers a gap that its related work names, earlier Cypher datasets "prepared independently", hard to use together: it merges 16 public sources, among them Neo4j Labs sets, CySpider and SyntheT2C, none a paper of this chapter [2]. The papers in [Further reading](../papers/graph-query-generation.md) build on it: Text2Cypher Across Languages translates its questions, Text2GraphQuery-Bench incorporates its dataset, LAST-CQ uses its benchmark, and CypherGuard, KG2Cypher and CypherTurn cite it [5][6][7][8][9][10]. Its text does not cite the Cypher language paper, [Francis et al. (2018)](./foundations.md) [2].

**Relations:** None.

**Read:** full text, arXiv HTML version 1, except numbers inside figures (20261102 20:41 UTC; the conclusion, Table 5 and the related-work passages re-read 20:52 UTC) [2]; arXiv record (20:40 UTC) [1]; ACL Anthology record (20:44 UTC) [3].

## Further reading

Papers checked against the four tests (origin, turning point, widely built on, survey of record) and left out of the fundamental list are marked with the reason. Counts are Semantic Scholar, read 20261102 19:47 UTC, carried over [4]; none is compared with a count from another source.

- **SPARQL-LLM** (2025), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14277>: a triplestore-agnostic SPARQL generator driven by endpoint metadata and curated query examples, deployed on bioinformatics graphs; v2 of 20260923 is marked as a TWEB article. It passes no test: it extends the authors' own 2024 preliminary work ("LLM-based SPARQL Query Generation from Natural Language over Federated Knowledge Graphs", arXiv 2410.06062 of 20241008, a retrieval-augmented system over bioinformatics graphs [15]), its count is **unstated**, and none of the six later papers read (LAST-CQ, KG2Cypher, CypherGuard, CypherTurn, Text2GraphQuery-Bench, SAGA) mentions it [11][13][6][7][8][9][10].
- **SPARQL-LLM in the TEXT2SPARQL 2026 Challenge** (2026), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13648>: the same system, reported first among eight approaches by its own authors; the scorer is unstated, so it is not independent evidence [12].
- **Text2Cypher Across Languages** (2025), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21445>: translates the Text2Cypher questions into Spanish and Turkish; performance falls from English to Spanish to Turkish, and multilingual fine-tuning narrows the gap. Two authors, Ozsoy and Tai, 3 citations, a narrow finding, so no test passed [5][4].
- **Text2GraphQuery-Bench** (2026), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11745>: 267,276 question-query pairs over 34 databases in Cypher, GQL and SQL/PGQ; 2 citations. A benchmark paper, so it belongs to the benchmarks chapter [10][4].
- **CypherTurn** (2026), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36987>: a multi-turn benchmark of 721 sessions; the best of 15 models reaches 64.7% execution accuracy and session-level correctness stays under 5%. EMNLP 2026 oral; a benchmark paper [9].
- **SAGA** (2026), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14494>: schema-aware grounding for agentic text-to-SPARQL; names the failure "type-blind grounding" [13].
- **KG2Cypher** (2026), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27742>: a data-centric pipeline for enterprise text-to-Cypher in Korean settings; first-party scores [7].
- **CypherGuard** (2026), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41755>: training-free detection and repair of structural faults in generated Cypher; 88.50% execution accuracy on CypherBench; one author, Ozsoy, is also the first author of Text2Cypher [8].
- **LAST-CQ** (2026), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12746>: a five-agent, training-free loop used to test which part of the repair loop helps; finds that detecting a failure and retrying matters more than the feedback's richness [6].
- **Learning to Refine** (2025), <https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11770>: a 3B model trained by outcome-driven reinforcement learning to build SPARQL in several steps [14].
