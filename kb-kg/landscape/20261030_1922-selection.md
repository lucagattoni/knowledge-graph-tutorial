# Selection: which projects get an entry

**Status: approved by the owner on 20261030, with a pilot first** (ten deep entries and one pilot batch of
four cards; the other card batches after the pilot was reviewed). Written 20261030 19:22 UTC from the snapshot
[`20261030_1913-github`](20261030_1913-github/README.md). The snapshot ranks by stars among
repositories pushed in the last 90 days and says it does not judge relevance. This file is the
judgement: which projects are about *LLMs and agents that create or query knowledge bases and
knowledge graphs*, and how deeply each is covered.

## The rule

- **In scope:** a project in one of the four categories that is starred and pushed within 90 days, or
  one that is quiet or archived but matters to the story (a July investigation, a retreat, a
  specification).
- **Deep entry** (about 200k tokens each in wave A): the ten that best combine momentum, a distinct
  direction, and closeness to a plain-files design. **Card:** everything else in scope.
- **Category is the editor's, not the tool's.** The tool files a project under the category whose
  queries returned it most often; where this file differs, this file wins.

## Gaps and cautions

- **The snapshot's recall is incomplete.** Seven projects I expected were not returned by its
  searches: codebase-memory-mcp (47,850 stars in my first pass), MIRIX, Zep, OntoGPT, GLiNER, DeepKE and
  the OKF specification repository. I added all seven to the watchlist, so the next snapshot tracks
  them. For now their numbers are from the first pass or unread, and each card writer reads live data.
  It follows that the snapshot cannot support a claim of the form "these are the most starred".
- **Stars are unvetted.** Nine repositories are flagged fast-growth; the largest is graphify at 664
  stars a day. The tool cannot tell organic growth from inflated growth, and the stargazer-timestamp
  endpoint returned 404 for at least one repository. Cards and deep entries report the flag and any
  evidence found, and do not conclude.
- **Category is partly arbitrary.** Several projects span two categories (memory layers that are also
  graph databases; wiki tools that are also memory). The category here follows the project's main
  claim.
- **Counts move.** Star counts differ by a few between the snapshot and a later read.
